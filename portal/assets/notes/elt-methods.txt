Approach, method and technique
An approach reflects beliefs about language and learning. A method is an organised teaching plan informed by those beliefs. A technique is a specific classroom procedure.


Recognise the classroom

• Grammar-Translation Method: explicit grammar rules, translation and emphasis on reading and writing.
• Direct Method: target-language use, oral interaction and meaning through demonstration or context.
• Audio-lingual Method: dialogue memorisation, imitation, repetition and pattern drills.
• Communicative Language Teaching: meaningful communication and communicative competence; language is used to complete purposes.
• Total Physical Response: learners respond physically to spoken commands, especially in early stages.

Example → identify the method
Teacher: “Stand up. Open the door.” Learners carry out the actions.
Total Physical Response.Learners have different train timetables and ask one another questions to plan a journey.
Communicative task: an information gap creates a reason to communicate.

Common exam trap
Communicative teaching values appropriate accuracy as well as fluency. It does not mean that grammar is never taught. A drill by itself is also not evidence of meaningful communication.


Revision cue
Translation → Grammar-Translation.
Repetition and patterns → Audio-lingual.
Meaning and purpose → Communicative.
Commands and actions → TPR.
