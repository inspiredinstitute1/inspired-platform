Figures of Speech Cheat Sheet

Prototype study note · Created for private review


Recognise the comparison
Simile makes an explicit comparison, often with like or as. Metaphor identifies one thing as another.
Simile: Her smile is like sunshine.
Metaphor: Her smile is sunshine.

Give life, exaggerate, or create contrast

• Personification: gives human qualities to something non-human. “The wind whispered.”
• Hyperbole: deliberate exaggeration. “I have told you a thousand times.”
• Oxymoron: places apparently contradictory terms together. “Deafening silence.”
• Paradox: a statement that seems contradictory but may express a truth. “Less is more.”

Listen to the sounds

• Alliteration: repetition of initial consonant sounds in nearby words. “Wild winds whistle.”
• Assonance: repetition of vowel sounds in nearby words. “Slow road.”
• Onomatopoeia: words that imitate sounds. “Buzz”, “hiss”, “clang”.

Common exam trap
Alliteration depends on sound, rather than just repeated letters. Also, the mere presence of “like” does not make a sentence a simile: “I like tea” contains no figurative comparison.
ಪದಗಳನ್ನು ಮಾತ್ರ ನೋಡಬೇಡಿ; ಅರ್ಥ ಮತ್ತು ಧ್ವನಿಯನ್ನು ಗಮನಿಸಿ.

Quick check
“The stars danced in the sky.” → Personification. Dancing is a human action attributed to stars.
