Subject–Verb Agreement

Prototype study note · Created for private review


Start with the subject
In the simple present, a third-person singular subject generally takes a verb with -s / -es. A plural subject takes the base form.
The student reads.
The students read.

Ignore the words between
A phrase between the subject and verb does not change the subject’s number.
The quality of these books is excellent.
“Books” is inside the prepositional phrase. The subject is “quality”.

Frequent exam patterns

• Each / every: use a singular verb. “Each of the students is ready.”
• One of + plural noun: the main verb agrees with “one”. “One of my friends has arrived.”
• A number of + plural noun: plural verb. “A number of students are absent.”
• The number of + plural noun: singular verb. “The number of students is increasing.”
• Either…or / neither…nor: in standard exam patterns with mixed number, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. “Neither the teacher nor the students are late.”

Common error
Incorrect: Each of the books are useful.
Correct: Each of the books is useful.
